**Abstract**

The relation between graphic differentiation and lexical derivation is central to interpreting Chinese writing as linguistic evidence. The received *Shuōwén jiězì* marks some characters as *yìshēng* 亦聲, indicating that a meaningful component also supplies the sound. We examine what lexical information this distinction adds by comparing 212 labelled entries with 953 ordinary phonetic compounds on the same 172 phonetics. Middle Chinese readings show an association with homophony (35.6% versus 15.5%; phonetic-clustered odds ratio 2.38). A blind semantic sample drawn from pairs with Old Chinese reconstructions also shows greater relatedness among labelled pairs (75.8% versus 12.0%). Within narrowly defined tone and voicing alternations, labelled pairs more often involve the departing tone; the evidence does not establish an additional association with which member carries that tone. Broader reconstructed affixal relations show no clear enrichment. Comparisons restricted to labels shared by the Dà Xú and Xiǎo Xú recensions retain the principal associations. We interpret *yìshēng* as a selective statement of lexical connection within graphic analysis. Such connections cross the boundary between differentiating the writing of a word or sense and representing a morphologically related word. This interpretation refines the relation between Wang Yun's graphic categories and linguistic relations, while distinguishing a label's value for identifying candidates from its ability to diagnose derivational direction or exhaust a word family.

**Keywords:** *Shuōwén jiězì*; *yìshēng*; Old Chinese morphology; graphic differentiation; lexical relatedness; Wang Yun

## 1. Introduction

Chinese characters preserve judgements about the relations between spoken words, but identifying the linguistic content of those judgements requires more than reading their component structure. A graph may be extended to another word, acquire a component that distinguishes one use, or be analysed retrospectively as a combination of meaningful parts. These processes can intersect with lexical derivation without reproducing its structure. The *Shuōwén jiězì* 說文解字, compiled by Xu Shen 許慎 around 100 CE and transmitted through later recensions, offers a particularly useful distinction: some phonetic components are explicitly described as meaningful as well. This paper asks what relations between words are associated with that distinction.

An ordinary phonetic compound typically receives an analysis of the form 从A，B聲, “from A, with B as phonetic”. In the formula 从A从B，B亦聲, both components enter the meaning-based analysis, and B is “also phonetic”, *yìshēng* 亦聲. The following labelled entries illustrate why the formula's linguistic interpretation cannot be read off its wording:

| Entry | Phonetic | Relation visible in the readings and glosses |
|---|---|---|
| 禮 *lejX*, “rite” | 豊 *lejX*, “ritual vessel” | Identical Middle Chinese readings, with a semantic connection in the analysis of ritual |
| 殯 *pjinH*, “to lay out the encoffined dead” | 賓 *pjin*, “guest” | A departing-tone contrast; the gloss explains the treatment of the dead through the treatment of a guest |
| 貧 *bin*, “poor” | 分 *pjun*, “to divide” | Differences in both initial and rhyme; the definition connects poverty with wealth being divided |

The label on 貧 occurs in the Dà Xú recension but not in the Xiǎo Xú, which gives plain 分聲. Wang Yun 王筠 accepted the label on 禮 and rejected that on 貧. The contrast therefore raises two connected questions: what linguistic relationship a label records, and how the transmitted analysis came to contain it.

Traditional and modern studies already offer substantial accounts of meaningful phonetics, graphic differentiation and the history of these analyses. Wang Yun's *Shuōwén shìlì* 說文釋例 (1837) distinguishes three kinds of *yìshēng*, including “differentiated graphs filed in their own section” (分別文之在本部者). His account connects an orthographic category with the allocation of uses to different graphs, but also prescribes where a label should occur and rejects some received analyses. Subsequent work examines phonetics' semantic and etymological functions and proposes corrections to the labelled inventory. The question pursued here concerns the distribution of linguistic relations across that inventory: relative to ordinary compounds sharing its phonetics, which relations does the label make more likely?

This comparison matters for the interpretation of Old Chinese morphology. Homophony may accompany the differentiation of written uses, but it may also accompany conversion or unrelated homophones. A departing-tone contrast may be relevant to historical suffixation, but neither the presence of such a contrast nor the component structure alone establishes a particular derivational history. Semantic connection supplies another dimension, although definitions in the same dictionary cannot be treated as wholly independent of its graphic analyses. We therefore compare sound, meaning and textual distribution while keeping their evidential roles distinct.

The analysis covers 212 labelled Dà Xú head entries and 953 ordinary compounds on the same phonetics. It finds a strong homophony association and, in a blind semantic subsample, a marked difference in relatedness. A restricted departing-tone relation is also enriched, whereas broader reconstructed regularity does not show a clear label effect. These results motivate an interpretation of *yìshēng* as selectively making lexical connections explicit in graphic analysis. We develop that interpretation through Wang Yun's categories, the separation of a tonal relation from its direction, and closely examined labelled and unlabelled examples. Its contribution is to clarify how graphic differentiation and lexical derivation intersect in this material, and which further conclusions the label can support.

### 2.4 Research questions and analytical comparisons

Three questions organise the study. First, which phonological and semantic relations distinguish labelled pairs from ordinary compounds using the same phonetics? Second, how do these relations connect graphic differentiation with lexical derivation, and what information does the label add to the graphic configuration? Third, how does the pattern vary with recension and filing practice? The second question is interpretive: its answer requires bringing the distributional results together with particular textual analyses rather than treating a numerical outcome as a direct measurement of word formation.

The comparisons retain the identifiers used in the released analysis: homophony (H1a), broader regular phonological relationships among non-identical pairs (H1b), the compound's departing tone within tone/voicing alternations (H1c), semantic relatedness (H2), and exploratory comparisons of recension (H3) and filing position (H4). H1b's Old Chinese category includes \*-s; it is not an independently delimited set of “other affixes”. H1c is further decomposed into whether a pair involves the departing tone and, conditional on that, which member carries it. This decomposition distinguishes two questions combined by the original outcome.

The hypotheses were refined after inspection of the same population and were not preregistered. The decomposition, the integrated interpretation and the additional within-series checks are exploratory. Reorganising their presentation does not turn them into independent confirmations. Neither a graphic account nor a root-based account is assigned a prediction of zero phonological or semantic association: those predictions would require additional assumptions about how the labels were chosen.

## 3. Data and analytical strategy

### 3.1 Texts and comparison pairs

The working source is the open digital Dà Xú *Shuōwén* maintained by the shuowenjiezi project, containing 9,833 head entries, Dà Xú fǎnqiè and Duan Yucai's commentary. A formula search returns 224 entries; three further cases, 從, U+20501 and 兩, were resolved manually. Four of the resulting 227 are false hits in which 亦 is itself the phonetic. They are reassigned to ordinary compounds on 亦. Eleven are *xīnfù* 新附 entries added by Xu Xuan 徐鉉 and excluded. This leaves 212 labelled entries on 172 phonetics. The same exclusions yield 953 ordinary compounds on those phonetics. Analyses concern an entry paired with its stated phonetic, not two words assumed in advance to stand in a base–derivative relation.

**Table 1.** Population, comparison group and phonological coverage

| Set or availability criterion | Labelled entries | Ordinary compounds |
|---|---:|---:|
| Raw Dà Xú formula matches, including three manual cases | 227 | — |
| After excluding 4 false hits and 11 *xīnfù* | 212 | 953 |
| Middle Chinese readings at both ends | 177 | 858 |

The *xīnfù* exclusion uses position at the end of a section and absence of Duan's commentary, which omits these additions. The heuristic identifies 398 entries in the full dictionary; 否, whose commentary appears under 不, was restored as a false positive. Formulas in variant-graph glosses and abbreviated phonetics (省聲) are excluded. The resulting population is defined by this digital text and extraction procedure.

The headword, relevant gloss and formula of 104 selected analysis entries were visually checked against the Waseda University Library scan of Chen Changzhi's 1873 edition, together with passages used in key quotations. This was a targeted spot check, not a random sample or complete collation. The 1963 Zhonghua edition reproduces the Chen edition, but our direct inspection was of the Waseda scan. The register records checked entries and page locations, including two unresolved or discrepant readings; details are retained in Supplementary Material S1. The remaining 108 analysis entries were not checked against the print in this round.

For the recension comparison, labelled entries were located in the Sibu congkan Xiǎo Xú *Shuōwén jiězì xìzhuàn* 說文解字繫傳, using the Kanseki Repository text and the National Library of China scan for omitted passages. The formula before Xu Kai's commentary (臣鍇曰) determined label status. Digital readings were checked against that text; scan transcriptions were checked by sampling. The final collation has 140 shared labels, 62 found only in the Dà Xú, and 10 undecidable cases. “Dà Xú-only” denotes the observed distribution, without assigning the direction or date of textual change.

Forty labelled entries are filed under their phonetic as section head; no ordinary compound in the dataset has that configuration. These are relevant to Wang Yun's filing convention, but filing position alone does not identify all differentiated graphs. We compare them with other labelled entries and examine whether their removal changes the main associations. Ordinary controls are selected from the labelled population's phonetics; comparisons using a restricted labelled population also restrict controls to the phonetics represented there.

### 3.2 Phonological and semantic measures

Dà Xú fǎnqiè were matched to *Guǎngyùn* 廣韻 readings in the tshet-uinh database and represented in Baxter–Sagart Middle Chinese notation. A pair is identical if its recorded syllables agree. A narrowly defined alternation changes only tone and/or initial voicing. Within that class, the departing tone may occur on the compound, on its phonetic, or on neither. The analysis also compares fǎnqiè strings directly, so the homophony result can be checked without the *Guǎngyùn* matching step.

Middle Chinese is used primarily for coverage and for observable reading relationships. It supplies both readings for 89% of pairs, compared with 27% for the Old Chinese dataset. These readings postdate Xu Shen. Using them to inform a historical interpretation assumes some continuity of the relations, not identity between Middle Chinese and Han pronunciation. Identical later readings can conceal earlier differences, and a later tonal contrast need not establish an early derivation. Direct fǎnqiè comparison addresses matching error, not this chronological issue.

Baxter–Sagart Old Chinese reconstructions, obtained through cddb, provide a complementary classification. Pairs are coded identical (I), or differing only in prefixes, suffixes, pharyngealisation or medial \*r (R), or by other initial, vowel or coda differences. R includes reconstructed \*-s and \*-ʔ as well as several proposed prefixes and other alternations. It measures a broad formal correspondence class; it does not establish the morphological function or direction of every member. Reconstructions also draw partly on phonetic-series evidence and therefore cannot serve as a wholly independent replication of the script-based comparison.

Meaning was assessed in a random sample of 100 pairs drawn from entries with Old Chinese forms at both ends. The sheet presented the two graphs and their *Shuōwén* definitions, with structural formulas removed and group membership concealed. The two authors independently assigned Y (same or near-synonymous basic meanings), E (a one-step extension), N (no relation), or X (proper name). Their assignments agreed on all 100 pairs before comparison; they jointly checked the final codes against the glosses. Five sampled entries were excluded as false hits or *xīnfù*, and one as a mispairing, 弈/廾. Removing proper names from the remaining 94 gives 83 pairs for the main semantic comparison.

Blindness to the label reduces one source of expectation, but the definitions remain part of the same lexicographic tradition as the analyses being evaluated. In particular, 58 of 212 labelled glosses contain the phonetic graph itself, compared with 11 of 953 ordinary entries. We flag these paronomastic glosses and repeat the comparisons without them. Their exclusion tests dependence on this conspicuous glossing pattern; subtler semantic shaping of a definition remains possible. The selected sampling frame also means that the semantic rates cannot be projected to all 212 labels.

A separate, nonblind descriptive classification covers the 196 Middle Chinese-identical pairs. The two authors reviewed categories L (an added determinative for the same word), F (a sense assigned its own graph), C (a conversion candidate), U (an unrelated homophone or phonetic loan), and X (proper name). These operational categories are not equivalent to Wang Yun's classification of the effect of added graphic components. In particular, conversion requires evidence about lexical category and usage beyond identity of pronunciation. We use the labels to organise candidate interpretations, without treating them as an independent test of those interpretations. The original coding files are preserved; the v7 example audit documents where abbreviated glosses leave a classification underdetermined.

### 3.3 Models and targeted checks

The original main models are logistic generalised estimating equations (GEE), with label status as predictor and observations clustered by phonetic under an exchangeable working correlation. Odds ratios are reported with 95% confidence intervals. The five original directional comparisons—H1a, the two H1b measures, H1c and H2—retain their one-sided Holm-adjusted p-values for traceability. Smaller comparisons use Fisher's exact test with the direction stated. These analyses describe an explored finite text population; significance is not treated as confirmation from new data.

GEE accounts for dependence within a series but does not remove differences between series' baseline rates. The earlier “matched phonetics” comparisons retained series represented in both groups and pooled their entries. Those checks restricted the comparison set but did not themselves condition on series. @@WITHIN_SERIES_METHOD@@

For presentation, the Middle Chinese pairs are partitioned into four mutually exclusive classes: identical; a narrow alternation involving the departing tone at either end; a narrow alternation not involving the departing tone; and other differences. This partition clarifies denominators and separates the presence of a departing-tone relationship from its orientation. The broader Old Chinese R class is reported separately because its coverage and definition differ. Existing checks exclude paronomastic glosses, the 40 entries filed under their phonetic, or Wang's nine rejected labels, and restrict the analysis to labels shared by both recensions. Each addresses a particular feature of data construction or transmission; their common inputs prevent treating them as fully independent replications.

## 4. The distribution of sound and meaning

### 4.1 Homophony and semantic relatedness

Labelled pairs are substantially more often identical in Middle Chinese: 63/177 (35.6%), compared with 133/858 (15.5%) among ordinary compounds. The GEE odds ratio is 2.38 (95% CI 1.69–3.34; Holm p = 1.2 × 10⁻⁶). The association remains when identity is measured directly from fǎnqiè strings and when overt paronomastic glosses are excluded (Table 2). Old Chinese identity shows the same direction on its smaller available sample, 15/60 versus 13/249; this is complementary evidence under the reconstruction's assumptions.

The semantic contrast is large within that frame: three quarters of the labelled non-name pairs are coded related, against approximately one eighth of ordinary pairs. Near-synonymy occurs in 8/33 labelled pairs and no ordinary pair in the sample. Removing glosses that explicitly contain the phonetic reduces the estimated association but leaves a sizeable difference. The result establishes relatedness as assessed from these definitions; it does not by itself establish cognation or semantic compositionality.

The sample also allows sound and meaning to be considered together. All nine homophonous labelled pairs are coded related, whereas none of the eight unrelated labelled pairs is homophonous. In a descriptive logistic model of identity using both label and relatedness, relatedness has OR 6.3 (p = 0.04) and label OR 1.4 (p = 0.69). The small selected sample does not support a causal mediation interpretation. It shows that the two observed associations should not be counted as entirely separate discoveries about the same pairs.

The larger nonblind classification gives compatible descriptive context. Among 63 identical labelled pairs, 42 were assigned F, 8 L, 5 C and 8 U. Among 133 ordinary identical pairs, 73 were assigned U and 42 X. These classifications identify the kinds of interpretation considered by the authors; the joint dependence on the glosses and visible labels limits their value as corroboration. Their role in the discussion is to guide close comparison of examples.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Graphic differentiation and word formation

The distribution gives *yìshēng* a recognisable linguistic profile. Relative to ordinary compounds on the same phonetics, labelled pairs are more often homophonous and, in the semantic sample, more often related in meaning. A restricted departing-tone relationship adds a further association. Together these findings support interpreting the formula as making some lexical connections explicit within graphic analysis. The relation so recorded is not a single word-formation operation: the same graphic device can distinguish uses of an existing word or represent a related word with a different linguistic history.

Wang Yun's categories help locate that intersection. His account asks what an added component does to an existing graph's use: preserve the meaning, separate a borrowed use, or allocate one meaning to a differentiated graph. Our phonological comparison asks a different question, whether the words paired through the graphic analysis have identical readings or show a particular formal contrast. These questions are related but need not yield the same classification. Wang treats both 傾, related to 頃, and 娶, related to 取, as differentiated graphs. The first pair has identical Middle Chinese readings; the second has a departing-tone contrast. Placing both within a graphic category leaves this linguistic distinction available for further investigation.

The descriptive L/F classification makes a similar distinction between adding a determinative and assigning a sense its own graph. Fifty of the 63 homophonous labelled pairs received those codes. This helps specify the interpretation being proposed, but cannot independently prove that fifty pairs are historically the same word: the classification uses the same definitions, was not blind, and does not track uses through dated contexts. In particular, a sense boundary in a dictionary is not automatically a word boundary. A historical account must examine how a use is distributed, whether it has distinct grammatical behaviour and how it is written beyond the dictionary.

Conversion exposes that problem especially clearly. The five C cases are candidates identified in the descriptive coding, not five independently demonstrated instances of zero derivation. A brief definition may describe an action using a noun, or a nominal use may coexist with a verbal one. Neither a modern translation nor unchanged pronunciation decides the relation. The current material therefore warrants distinguishing possible category change from same-word respelling, while leaving the stronger analysis to usage evidence. This preserves the relevance of conversion to the research question without making graphic differentiation its diagnostic.

The 賓 series brings the distinctions together. 殯 *pjinH* is labelled; 賓 *pjin*, “guest”, enters its definition through treating the coffined dead as a guest. The ordinary compounds 儐 and 鬢 are also read *pjinH*. 鬢, “hair on the cheeks”, has no comparable connection with guests in its definition. 儐 is more informative, but requires care: the *Shuōwén* gloss is 導也, “to guide”, whereas Duan distinguishes an ushering sense from the sense of treating someone with the rites due to a guest. His latter interpretation connects 儐 with 賓 while revising the received gloss. The unlabelled 儐 thus provides a candidate lexical connection on a stated exegetical analysis, rather than an independently established derivation concealed in an unambiguous definition.

This comparison shows what sound alone cannot do. Three similar tonal relationships do not entail the same semantic or morphological analysis. Conversely, the presence of a plausible semantic connection does not guarantee a transmitted *yìshēng* formula. The label is useful for locating relations that deserve investigation, while its exceptions are part of the phenomenon to be explained. They prevent turning a distributional tendency into a definition of every labelled entry.

### 5.2 What the label adds

The departing-tone decomposition identifies an important limit on the label's added information. The original comparison asked whether the compound is the departing-tone member more often in labelled pairs. That outcome combines the frequency of a tonal relationship with its orientation. Once those components are separated, the clearer association concerns involvement of the departing tone. The observed orientation is similar in the two groups, and its estimates do not establish an additional label effect. A common position of the departing-tone member in graphic configurations should therefore not be attributed specifically to *yìshēng*.

Nor does that position establish a historical direction of derivation. The added graphic component, the occurrence of a tonal reflex and the formation of a new word belong to different histories. Boltz's account of graphic extension and disambiguation provides reasons to examine those histories together; it also includes paths involving borrowing between words without a semantic relation. It cannot supply the missing derivation for each pair. The present result supports a relation-sensitive use of the label: it helps select pairs with a particular phonological profile, without deciding all steps by which those pairs arose.

Several simpler accounts of the associations can be constrained. Removing glosses that contain the phonetic excludes the most direct form of definitional reinforcement, yet the homophony and semantic differences remain. Direct fǎnqiè identity preserves the homophony association without the matching procedure. Restricting the labels to the shared recension inventory shows that a Dà Xú-only subset is not sufficient to explain the whole pattern. These checks make a lexical interpretation worth pursuing because the association survives changes directly relevant to its measurement and textual support.

Their limits also indicate what the explanation must accommodate. Glosses can express a preferred semantic connection without naming the phonetic, readings can preserve or obscure relations across time, and both recensions can inherit an earlier analysis. A plausible working account is that the formula selectively makes a component's lexical meaning available in explaining the compound, while its use depends on the conventions of graphic analysis and transmission. This account accommodates related unlabelled pairs and variation in formula wording. Whether perceived semantic accessibility determines the selection would require an independently assessed measure of that accessibility; renaming the observed label distribution as “transparency” would add no explanation.

### 5.3 Transmission and Wang Yun's convention

The received labels should be understood as a textual distribution before they are attributed to an individual analyser. Wang Yun's observation that the two recensions often differ is borne out by the collation. His additional judgement that particular Dà Xú readings are mistaken additions requires separate historical and philological arguments. A formula present in only one recension may reflect addition, omission or a more complicated transmission history. The present alignment identifies the difference but does not by itself choose between those histories.

Wang's normative account is also more specific than a general opposition between related and unrelated words. He rejects labels on some differentiated graphs when the semantic contribution is an extended or borrowed use, and when his filing convention calls for a plain phonetic. Several rejected examples nevertheless instantiate the close sound relations enriched in the population. That is not a contradiction between his evidence and ours: his judgement about an appropriate analysis and our comparison of reading relationships address different properties.

The nonblind classifications help locate the mismatch. Of the 50 homophonous labelled pairs assigned L or F, 42 are filed outside their phonetic's section. That distribution includes both Dà Xú-only and shared labels. The close reading of Wang's accepted, rejected and unchallenged examples likewise shows that a single filing rule does not straightforwardly describe all received uses. This supports separating the lexical insight in his discussion of differentiation from the convention he uses to evaluate particular formulas. The nonsignificant H4 comparison alone could not establish that separation; the interpretation also depends on what his rule says and on the cases to which it is applied.

Dà Xú-only labels have a higher homophony rate than shared labels, and many of their homophonous pairs were descriptively classified as sense differentiation. An expansion of explicit analysis to such uses is consequently one possible account of the difference. It remains a hypothesis about transmission, not a demonstrated act of Xu Xuan. The principal associations in shared labels show that the linguistic profile is broader than the Dà Xú-only difference. They cannot date that profile to Xu Shen's autograph or establish the intentions of either editor.

### 5.4 Implications for Old Chinese morphology and yòuwén

The study's morphological implication is a distinction among the tasks assigned to written evidence. Graphic analysis can identify a pair worth investigating. Independent reading information and carefully interpreted definitions can strengthen a proposed lexical connection. Establishing a particular derivation then requires evidence of its form, function and historical relationship. The label contributes to that sequence by selecting informative candidates within phonetic series; its value does not depend on representing a complete morphological analysis.

This distinction connects the Chinese case to the problem of structural diagnostics raised by Arad (2003) and Rasin, Preminger and Pesetsky (2024). A label external to the present analysis avoids defining the groups by the phonological result being tested. It does not thereby become an independent indication that one form is built on a categorized word rather than a root. Likewise, related dictionary meanings are not a direct test of the inheritance of a base's interpretation. Word-based descriptions can organise the candidate relations economically, but the data do not discriminate a general word-based architecture from a root-based one. The useful theoretical question is which additional observations would permit that discrimination for the particular pairs.

The same reasoning clarifies the relation to *yòuwén* 右文 and the source-indicating phonetic. A phonetic series is a graphic comparison set whose members may include semantically connected words, borrowed graphs and unrelated homophones. Modern accounts of meaningful phonetics already distinguish several of these routes. Our contribution is to measure how a particular transmitted formula partitions such a set and to show that this partition carries linguistic information. The 6/50 related ordinary pairs in the semantic sample also matter: an unlabelled pair cannot simply be declared unrelated. Neither that proportion nor the labelled rate estimates systematicity across the Chinese lexicon; the units and sampling differ from lexicon-wide sound–meaning studies.

Three remaining limits shape the scope of this interpretation. First, the semantic evidence describes an Old Chinese-covered sample and definitions within the received lexicographic tradition. Second, the reading relationships are temporally layered, and the narrow tonal measure excludes possible \*-s relations with entering-tone or rhyme differences. Third, the graphic population and some collation readings have been checked only selectively. These limits call for focused extensions: sentence-level evidence for the conversion candidates, dated written variants for proposed differentiation, and additional phonological correspondences for individual derivations. They do not remove the observed distinction between the labelled and ordinary sets, but determine how far it can be carried into a history of words.

## 6. Conclusion

The received *Shuōwén*'s *yìshēng* formula is associated with lexical information beyond the fact that two entries share a phonetic component. Labelled pairs more often have identical readings, their meanings are more often connected in the blind subsample, and narrowly defined alternations more often involve the departing tone. The broader regularity measures provide no clear evidence for general affixal enrichment, while the tonal decomposition supplies no demonstrated additional directional value for the label.

These findings place *yìshēng* at the intersection of graphic differentiation and lexical relatedness. They refine Wang Yun's account by distinguishing the linguistic relations within a graphic category from his filing and editorial conventions. They also specify how the label can enter morphological research: as a selective source of candidates whose lexical identity, grammatical relationship and history require further evidence. A useful next step is to examine dated contextual uses of those candidates, particularly where identical readings could accompany either sense differentiation or conversion.
