**Abstract**

How can derivational relations be identified when derivational morphology is largely absent from the written record? We ask whether a native analyst's classification can serve as evidence, and of which part of a derivational relation. The *Shuōwén jiězì* (100 CE) analyses about two hundred characters as *yìshēng* 亦聲, their meaningful component being "also the phonetic". We compare all 212 labelled entries of the Dà Xú recension with the 953 ordinary phonetic compounds on the same 172 phonetics, using Middle Chinese readings, Old Chinese reconstructions and a semantic coding made blind to the label. Labelled characters are more than twice as often homophonous with their phonetic (35.6% vs 15.5%) and are enriched in departing-tone alternations, the reflex of the Old Chinese suffix \*-s, but not in other alternations of tone or voicing. They are far more often related in meaning (76% vs 12%), and the label carries no detectable information about which member is derived. The sound effects are carried mainly by labels found only in the Dà Xú and by entries whose gloss uses the phonetic; in the labels most likely to be Xu Shen's own, the contrast in meaning stays large while that in sound is weak. We argue that the label is evidence of lexical relatedness, which in Old Chinese phonetic series is densest among pairs that are homophonous or differ by \*-s, and that where morphology is covert, relatedness, formal relation and direction must be diagnosed separately.

**Keywords** Lexical relatedness · Derivation · Conversion · Old Chinese · Suffix \*-s · Writing systems · Metalinguistic evidence

## 1 Introduction

Derivational relations are usually diagnosed from overt form: a shared stem, a recurrent affix, a regular change of meaning. Where the form gives no sign, as in conversion, relatedness and direction have to be argued on other grounds (e.g., Ševčíková & Hledíková, 2026), and the diagnostics proposed for such cases do not always hold up (Rasin et al., 2024). Old Chinese presents the problem in an extreme form. Its affixes are reconstructed rather than observed, several of them left no trace in the Middle Chinese syllable, and the script that is its main record writes whole syllables, so that no affix is ever written on its own. This paper asks whether evidence of an independent kind, a native analyst's classification, can diagnose derivation, and if so, which part of it.

Most Chinese characters are phonetic compounds (形聲字), which join a semantic element to a phonetic element that is itself a character; compounds that share a phonetic form a phonetic series. The *Shuōwén jiězì* 說文解字 of Xu Shen 許慎 (100 CE; hereafter *Shuōwén*) analyses most of them with the formula 从A，B聲 'from A; B gives the sound'. For about two hundred it writes instead 从A从B，B亦聲 'from A and from B; B also gives the sound', so that B contributes to the meaning as well. This is the *yìshēng* 亦聲 ('also phonetic') label. The pronunciations that can be compared directly are those of Middle Chinese (MC, c. 600 CE), recorded in rhyme books; Old Chinese (OC) forms are reconstructions, which rest partly on the phonetic series themselves. The MC departing tone (去聲) goes back largely to an OC suffix \*-s. The series of 賓 *pjin* 'guest' shows what is at stake. 殯 *pjinH* 'to lay out the encoffined dead', whose gloss says the dead is "treated as a guest" (賓遇之), is analysed 从歺从賓，賓亦聲. 鬢 *pjinH* 'hair on the cheeks' stands in exactly the same relation of sound to 賓 and is an ordinary compound. Does the label record a relation between graphs, between meanings, or between words?

Wang Yun 王筠 came closest to the last answer. His *Shuōwén shìlì* 說文釋例 (1837) counts "differentiated graphs filed in their own section" among the kinds of *yìshēng* and rejects nine labels as additions to the Dà Xú 大徐 recension (Section 2.1). But he counted nothing and compared no sounds.

We test the label on its whole population, comparing every *yìshēng* entry of the Dà Xú text with the ordinary compounds on the same phonetics against evidence gathered independently of the label: MC readings, OC reconstructions and a semantic coding made blind to it. The label covaries with this evidence, selectively. Labelled pairs are enriched in identity of sound and in the departing-tone alternation, not in the other alternations found in the same series; they are far more often related in meaning; and they carry no detectable information about which member is derived. In the labels most likely to be Xu's own, however, the sound effects weaken while the relation of meaning does not.

The paper makes three contributions. It gives Wang's account its first population-level, phonologically controlled test. It shows how a native classification can be validated like any other, against a within-series baseline and independent evidence, and finds that what this one tracks is lexical relatedness, densest among homophones and \*-s pairs, rather than derivation with a direction. And it argues that relatedness, formal relation and direction, which overt morphology supplies together, must be diagnosed separately where morphology is covert. Section 2 sets out the background and four rival accounts, which Sections 3 to 5 test.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Native analyses: from Duan Yucai to Wang Yun

In the Dà Xú recension, 227 head entries carry the formula 从A从B，B亦聲; Section 3.1 explains why 212 are analysed. Duan Yucai (1815) read the label as marking a graph whose phonetic also contributes meaning: "whenever [Xu] says 'also phonetic', the character is a meaning-compound that is at the same time a phonetic compound" (凡言亦聲者，會意兼形聲也). He also thought Xu had used it too sparingly. 論 'discuss' "should read" 侖亦聲, and 128 characters are "phonetic compounds containing a meaning-compound" (形聲包會意), 203 with related wordings. The label was thus the visible edge of the *yòuwén* 右文 principle, recorded since Shen Kuo (ca. 1090/2016), that characters sharing a phonetic mostly, or in Duan's bolder formulas all, share its meaning (Xu Shushi, 2024; Zeng, 2002). Shen Jianshi (1935) separated this claim about whole series from the claim about a single pair (形聲兼會意).

Later work has asked which characters the label should cover. Wang Li (1982, as cited in Zeng, 2002) held all *yìshēng* characters to be cognate with their phonetic. Zeng (2002) objected that some are merely accumulated graphs of the same word. Li Guoying (1996/2020), whose school named the phonetic's "source-indicating function" (示源功能), argues that a graph differentiated from its source inherits the source's sound, whereas a derived word "need not be" homophonous with its base. Li Ning and Guo (2019) count 217 labels and claim that 79 more characters deserve one, but their criterion already includes derivation from the phonetic and Old Chinese homophony. Hsu (2005) collects 267 labelled characters from three versions of the text. A recent review faults the programme for "substituting the character for the word" (以字代詞; Chen Shuo, 2022).

Wang Yun's *Shuōwén shìlì* (1837) makes a different kind of claim, and it is the one tested here. The label "is said of three kinds: meaning-compounds that also give the sound; phonetic compounds that also give the meaning; and differentiated graphs filed in their own section" (言亦聲者凡三種：會意字而兼聲者，一也；形聲字而兼意者，二也；分別文之在本部者，三也; Wang Yun, 1837, juan 3). A differentiated graph (分別文) adds a component that changes the meaning of an existing graph; one that leaves the meaning unchanged is an accumulated graph (累增字; juan 8). A filing rule links the third kind to the label. Differentiated graphs filed in another section never state the base's meaning, while those in their own section make the principal component the sound as well (在異部者，概不言義；在本部者，概以主義兼聲也), as with 胖 'half a carcass', filed under 半 'half' rather than 肉 'meat'. Wang also pruned the transmitted labels. Of thirteen labelled characters, he accepts four (禮, 祏, 胖, 柵) and rejects nine (貧, 愾, 恇, 娶, 婚, 姻, 婢, 緉, 坪) as "mistaken additions in the Dà Xú" (大徐誤增). Yet in juan 8 he quotes three labelled graphs filed away from their base (傾 'lean', 𨻺 and 䫇) without objection. Wang thus tied one kind of *yìshēng*, not the label as a whole, to graph differentiation. Neither he nor later scholars asked how often the label coincides with a relation between words, against what baseline, or with which difference in sound.

### 2.2 Derivation in Old Chinese

Historical phonology says which differences in sound a derivation would leave. Karlgren (1933) grouped Old Chinese words into families linked by recurrent alternations, without analysing these as affixes; Pulleyblank (1973, 2000) did, and current reconstructions posit an explicit system of derivational affixes (Baxter & Sagart, 1998, 2014; Sagart, 1999; Schuessler, 2007). The best established is the suffix \*-s, the source of the Middle Chinese departing tone. It most often formed nouns from verbs and adjectives (Baxter & Sagart, 1998; Mei, 2012), but it also formed verbs, and it has been split into three suffixes (Baxter & Sagart, 2014), traced to several merged ones (Jacques, 2016; Zhang, 2022), or reduced to a marker of outward-directed derivation (Schuessler, 2007). All these accounts treat the departing-tone member as the derived one. Alternations of initial voicing, the second main type, are attributed to a prefix \*N- (Sagart & Baxter, 2012).

Native evidence enters in two ways. Jacques (2022) defends the tone-change readings (破讀) of Han to early Tang commentators, collected in the *Jīngdiǎn shìwén*, as evidence of inherited morphology; such readings are attested from Xu Shen's own time (Zhang, 2022). The script, too, often writes a derived word with its base's graph, as Warring States manuscripts write 義 'duty' as 宜 'proper' (Baxter & Sagart, 2014), or adds a semantic element to that graph, as in 授 'give' beside 受 'receive', already distinct on the oracle bones (Jacques, 2022). Character formation has been modelled as evidence for Old Chinese phonology (Hill & List, 2019), but the *Shuōwén*'s labels have not, to our knowledge, been tested as evidence of derivation.

### 2.3 Graphs, words and the diagnosis of derivation

Whether a label on a graph can say anything about words depends on how phonetic compounds arose. Boltz (1994) derives most of them from a graph borrowed for a homophonous, usually unrelated word and then given a semantic determinative, so that word families within a series are "an accidental feature"; yet he reads Xu's formula as giving the phonetic "a simultaneous phonetic and semantic function". Qiu (2000) traces such meaningful phonetics to graphs used in an extended sense and then given a semantic element, as in 娶 'take a wife' from 取 'take'. He finds them a small share of all compounds and rejects the claim that a phonetic's meaning is shared by all its compounds (see also Schuessler, 2007).

Morphological theory meets the same problem wherever derivation has no overt marker. Arad (2003) distinguishes words formed from roots from words formed from existing words, which keep their base's meaning, and diagnoses the latter by formal traces of the base and entailment of its meaning. Rasin et al. (2024) find that the two diagnostics come apart in her own Hebrew and English data, so formal and semantic evidence of derivation must be established independently. Lexeme-based work places lexical meaning in words rather than roots (Aronoff, 2007), and paradigm-based work defines relatedness as a content relation and a form relation that recur together across pairs, with no single base required (Bonami & Strnadová, 2019; Hathout & Namer, 2019). A *yìshēng* label is a third, native kind of evidence: a record of the pairs in which an ancient analyst saw the phonetic enter the meaning. Whether it also tracks form, beyond what meaning predicts, is an empirical question.

One baseline remains. Form and meaning are weakly associated even without morphology (Dingemanse et al., 2015; Monaghan et al., 2014), in Chinese rhyme books as elsewhere (Meng et al., 2025), although that study did not separate phonetic series from other homophones. Any effect of the label must therefore be measured within phonetic series, against their unlabelled members.

### 2.4 Four accounts and their predictions

Four accounts of the label make different predictions (Table 1).

(A) On a meaning-first account, the label records that the phonetic contributes meaning, as Duan read it, and carries no information about sound of its own. It still predicts a sound profile, through meaning. A graph differentiated from its source inherits the source's reading (Li Guoying, 1996/2020), so labelled pairs should share the sound profile of the semantically related pairs in their series, whatever that profile turns out to be.

(B) If the label is a general, implicit marker of derivation, the hypothesis this study started from, labelled pairs should be enriched in identity (conversion, sense specialisation), in \*-s and in the other affixal relations alike, and the label should single out the derived member.

(C) If the label marks a new graph for the same word or for a word minimally derived from it, an extension of Wang's differentiated graph, identity and \*-s should be enriched and other affixal relations not, and the label need say nothing about direction. Unlike (A), it predicts that among pairs related in meaning, labelled ones are more often identical or \*-s related.

(D) If the effect reflects lexicon-wide systematicity, labelled and ordinary members of the same series should differ little.

**Table 1** Predictions of four accounts of the label

| | (A) Meaning first | (B) General derivation | (C) Same word or minimal derivative | (D) Systematicity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identity of sound (H1a) | +^a^ | + | + | small |
| Departing-tone alternation (H1c, step 1) | +^a^ | + | + | small |
| Other alternations (H1b without the departing tone) | 0^a^ | + | 0 | small |
| Relatedness of meaning (H2) | + | + | + | small |
| Direction of \*-s pairs | – | label picks the derived member | no difference | – |
| Sound among related pairs | no difference | + | + | – |

*Note.* + labelled pairs enriched; 0 no difference; – no prediction. ^a^ Predicted through meaning, to the extent that related members of a series are identical or \*-s related.

(A) and (C) part company only in the last row, and testing it needs pairs related in meaning on both sides of the label. The analysis is organised by four directional hypotheses and two exploratory comparisons:

- **H1a (homophony).** Labelled characters are homophonous with their phonetic more often than ordinary compounds on the same phonetics.
- **H1b (other regular relations).** Among non-homophonous pairs, labelled characters differ from their phonetic by a regular alternation or affix more often.
- **H1c (the departing tone).** Among pairs that differ only in tone or voicing, the labelled character is more often the departing-tone (\*-s) member.
- **H2 (meaning).** Labelled characters are related in meaning to their phonetic more often, even without glosses that define a character by its own phonetic.
- **H3 (recensions; exploratory).** Labels on which the Dà Xú, the Xiǎo Xú 小徐 and Duan's text disagree differ from stable labels on H1a.
- **H4 (Wang's filing criterion; exploratory).** Labelled characters filed under their own phonetic differ on H1a from other labelled characters.

The hypotheses were refined after an initial look at this population and were not preregistered; Table 1 gives the order of presentation, not a record of prior prediction. H1c as stated combines (B)'s claim about the derived member with (C)'s claim about \*-s, and Section 4.2 separates the two.

## 3 Data and method

### 3.1 Population and comparison group

The working text is the open digital *Shuōwén* of the shuowenjiezi project (GitHub, Apache-2.0), with 9,833 head entries, the Dà Xú fǎnqiè and Duan's commentary. A pattern search finds the formula in 224 head entries, and three more (從, 𠔁, 兩) were resolved by hand. Of these 227, four are false hits in which 亦 is itself the phonetic, and eleven are *xīnfù* 新附 characters added by Xu Xuan 徐鉉 in 986, identified as section-final entries that lack Duan's commentary. What remains is 212 labelled entries on 172 phonetics (Table 2). We visually checked the headword, gloss and formula of 104 of them, together with the passages quoted in examples, against the Waseda University Library scan of Chen Changzhi's 1873 edition; this was a targeted check, not a random sample or a complete collation.

The comparison group consists of all 953 ordinary compounds (B聲) on the same 172 phonetics after the same exclusions. Restricting it to series with a labelled member matters, because homophony with the phonetic is far more likely within a series than between random pairs. Forty labelled entries are filed in the section headed by their own phonetic, as 胖 is under 半. By Wang's rule these are the candidates for his third kind (H4), but their label also follows from Xu's filing system, a section head being by definition a meaningful component, so every main model is also run without them.

For H3, each label was collated with the Xiǎo Xú recension (*Shuōwén jiězì xìzhuàn* 說文解字繫傳) in the Sibu congkan edition, read in its digital text (Kanseki Repository KR1j0019) and, where that text is incomplete, in the National Library of China scan. Of the 212 labels, 140 appear in both recensions and 62 in the Dà Xú alone. Ten cannot be decided: seven lie in juan 25, which the Xiǎo Xú lost and later supplied from the Dà Xú; two (詔, 糶) were probably supplied the same way; and the line of one (瑁) is blank in the print. We treat a Dà Xú label as Xu Shen's judgement unless the collation shows otherwise. This is the first of three bridging assumptions, and the 140 shared labels test it against the Song editors, though not against earlier transmission.

### 3.2 Sound

The primary tests use MC. Each character's Dà Xú fǎnqiè 反切, a spelling of its syllable by two other characters, was matched against the readings of the *Guǎngyùn* 廣韻 (1008), the standard rhyme book, in the open tshet-uinh database (nk2028), and written in Baxter's transcription (Baxter & Sagart, 2014), in which a final -X marks the rising tone and -H the departing tone. Each pair of member and phonetic then falls into one of four mutually exclusive categories: identical syllables; a departing-tone alternation (a difference only in tone and/or initial voicing, with one member in the departing tone); another tone or voicing alternation; or any other difference. For departing-tone alternations we record which member carries the departing tone. Identity was also coded directly from the two fǎnqiè strings, which bypasses the *Guǎngyùn* matching.

MC comes first for coverage, since readings exist at both ends for 89% of pairs and OC reconstructions for only 27%, and to avoid circularity, since some OC forms are reconstructed "on the basis of graphic or word-family connections" (Baxter & Sagart, 2014). It rests on the second bridging assumption: that the relations visible in the MC readings, identity and a difference of tone, reflect relations between the words Xu analysed. Some tone-change readings may be later, analogical creations of the reading tradition (Baxter & Sagart, 1998), although Jacques (2022) argues that the tradition as a whole preserves inherited morphology.

OC forms (Baxter & Sagart, 2014, from the open cddb database; square brackets mark uncertain segments) type the relations that MC does not show. Each pair was classed as identical (I); different only in prefixes, suffixes (including \*-ʔ and \*-s), pharyngealisation or medial \*r (R); or different in the initial (O, O2), the vowel (V) or the coda (C). Because R includes \*-s, we also split it by whether the difference involves \*-s.

### 3.3 Meaning

Xu wrote the gloss and assigned the label in a single act, which is the central difficulty for any semantic test. In 58 of the 212 labelled entries (27.4%) the gloss contains the phonetic character itself, as in 政 正也; among ordinary compounds the figure is 11 of 953 (1.2%). We flag these paronomastic glosses and rerun every test without them. For H2 the check removes a leak, since a coder who sees the phonetic in the gloss may code the pair as related for that reason alone. For H1 it asks whether the sound relations persist where the gloss does not name the phonetic; since such a gloss is the most explicit form a relation between two words can take, some weakening is expected on any account that ties the label to such relations.

Meaning was coded on a random sample of 100 pairs (40 labelled, 60 ordinary) drawn from the pairs with OC forms at both ends. For each pair the sheet shows the phonetic character and the member character with their glosses, stripped of 从某, 某聲 and 某亦聲, and the relation between their basic meanings (本義) is coded Y (same or near-synonymous), E (one step of extension), N (none) or X (proper name). The pairs were coded twice by a large language model (Claude, Anthropic). The first pass was made by the model instance that built the dataset and knew each pair's group. The second was made blind by a separate instance, which received only the coding instructions and the glosses, stripped of Xu's structural analysis, and was barred from consulting files or the web (the protocol is in the repository). The two passes agree on 86 of the 100 pairs (Cohen's κ = 0.773). None of the 14 disagreements sets Y against N: six fall between Y and E, five concern whether a character is a proper name, and three fall between E and N. We analyse the blind pass throughout; the first pass gives the same contrast (27 of 35 labelled against 8 of 53 ordinary pairs related). Both authors then coded the pairs independently from the unlabelled sheet and agreed with the blind pass on every pair; their working sheets were not kept, so this check is reported but cannot be reproduced from the released files. Both machine sheets are released. Because the two passes come from one model family, κ measures the consistency of the procedure, not agreement between independent human judges.

Six items were later excluded (five *xīnfù* or false hits, one mispairing), leaving 94, and H2 sets aside 11 proper names, using 83. Four ordinary pairs lie in series whose only label is a *xīnfù*; without them H2 gives OR 20.2, and the comparisons involving sound in Section 4.3 use the other 79. The glosses stand for Han-period relations of meaning as Xu understood them, not for modern etymology; this is the third bridging assumption.

A further coding, made with the label visible and used only descriptively, assigned each of the 196 MC-homophonous pairs a relation type: the same word with an added determinative (累增字, L), one sense given its own graph (分別文, F), conversion (C), an unrelated homophone or loan (U), or a proper name (X). Both authors reviewed it pair by pair. The types borrow Wang's terms but not his criteria: ours classify the relation between two words, his the effect of the added component on the graph, so that 傾, a differentiated graph for Wang, is L in our coding.

### 3.4 Models

Each binary outcome is modelled by logistic generalised estimating equations (GEE) with the label as predictor, clustered by phonetic under an exchangeable working correlation. The five directional tests (H1a; H1b in MC and in OC; H1c; H2) are one-sided and Holm-adjusted. GEE corrects the uncertainty for dependence within series, but it does not remove differences between series in their baseline rate of homophony. We therefore also compare labelled and ordinary members within series, pooling over the phonetics that have both with a Mantel–Haenszel (MH) odds ratio and a Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test. Where cells are sparse, as for the direction of \*-s pairs, we use a conditional maximum-likelihood odds ratio with an exact interval, and Fisher's exact tests describe small subsets and the descriptive comparisons of Sections 4.4 and 4.5. All secondary comparisons are two-sided and unadjusted, and all tests describe this population rather than confirm predictions made in advance (Section 2.4). Models were fitted in Python (statsmodels).

## 4 Results

### 4.1 The sound profile (H1a)

H1a holds (Table 3). More than a third of labelled pairs are homophonous, against 15.5% of ordinary pairs (OR 2.38, 95% CI 1.69–3.34; Holm p = 1.2 × 10⁻⁶), and the contrast barely shrinks within series (MH OR 2.15). Departing-tone alternations are also enriched, less strongly (GEE OR 1.64; within series 2.05). The other alternations are, if anything, rarer among labelled pairs, and so is the residual category.

| Identical | 63 (35.6%) | 133 (15.5%) | 2.38 [1.69, 3.34] | 2.15 [1.36, 3.42] |
| Departing-tone alternation | 21 (11.9%) | 58 (6.8%) | 1.64 [1.04, 2.57] | 2.05 [1.03, 4.11] |

The OC forms, on far smaller numbers, show the same contrast more sharply: 15 of 60 labelled pairs are identical (25.0%), against 13 of 249 ordinary ones (5.2%).

### 4.2 Which alternations? (H1b, H1c)

Where member and phonetic differ only in tone or voicing, the labelled character is the departing-tone member in 16 of 33 pairs (48.5%) and the ordinary compound in 43 of 150 (28.7%; OR 2.44, 95% CI 1.34–4.48; Holm p = 0.0056). Among the labelled cases, all shared by both recensions and glossed without the phonetic, are 琀 *homH* 'jade placed in the mouth of the dead' beside 含 *hom* 'to hold in the mouth', 珥 *nyiH* 'ear-jade' beside 耳 *nyiX* 'ear', and 憙 *xiH* 'to delight in' beside 喜 *xiX* 'joy'. The result holds without the 40 labels filed under their phonetic and, imprecisely, within series (MH OR 7.35 on 22 series; Table 7).

H1c, however, bundles two claims (Table 4). The first, that labelled alternations involve the departing tone more often, holds (OR 2.42). The second, that the label picks out the derived member, finds no support. Once a pair involves the departing tone, the compound is the departing-tone member in 76% of labelled and 74% of ordinary pairs (conditional OR 1.11, 95% CI 0.31–4.57). The interval is wide, and a moderate difference cannot be excluded. Pooled, compounds are the departing-tone member in 59 of these 79 pairs (75%), more often than where one member has the departing tone but the syllables also differ in other ways, 91 of 150 (61%; Fisher p = 0.04). Only that difference can be credited to derivation, and the 61% may itself include \*-s derivatives with further changes.

H1b fails as stated. Tone and voicing alternations are only somewhat commoner among non-identical labelled pairs (28.9% against 20.7%; Holm p = 0.20), and the OC measure shows nothing (Holm p = 0.31). Taken apart, however, the class is informative (Table 4). Among non-identical pairs, the MC excess lies entirely in the departing-tone alternations (OR 2.12, 95% CI 1.38–3.26), while the remaining alternations sit at or below baseline (OR 0.70, 95% CI 0.36–1.36), as do voicing alternations and level–rising contrasts taken separately (6 of 114 against 35 of 725, and 5 against 48); the upper limit leaves room for at most a small enrichment. The other affixes of (B), though, mostly surface in MC as differences of initial or rhyme, outside these categories. The direct test of them is the OC class R without \*-s, which shows no excess but says less (OR 0.81, 95% CI 0.39–1.65). Relations involving \*-s trend upward (OR 2.01, 95% CI 0.98–4.10), but they are largely the MC departing-tone pairs seen again.

Sound and meaning go together. All nine homophonous labelled pairs in the subsample are related in meaning. In each group, related pairs are more often identical or departing-tone alternants than unrelated ones (labelled 14 of 25 against 0 of 8, Fisher p = 0.01; ordinary 2 of 6 against 4 of 40, p = 0.17); pooling the groups, 10 of 13 homophonous pairs and 6 of 7 departing-tone pairs are related, against 1 of 6 other alternations and 14 of 53 pairs that differ in other ways. Among related pairs, by contrast, the label goes with sound only weakly (14 of 25 against 2 of 6; p = 0.39), and in a descriptive model, relatedness predicts identity or a departing-tone alternation (OR 10.1, p = 0.003) while the label adds little once relatedness is known (OR 1.3, p = 0.74). The descriptive relation types agree. Of the 63 homophonous labelled pairs, 42 are F, 8 L, 5 C and only 8 U; among the 133 homophonous ordinary pairs, unrelated homophones (73) and proper names (42) predominate, with 15 F, 3 L and no C.

### 4.4 How much of the label?

Identity exceeds the ordinary rate by 20 percentage points and the departing-tone alternation by 5 (Table 3), so the pairs that set the label apart from ordinary compounds make up only about a quarter of those labelled. Nearly half (81 of 177) differ in more than tone or voicing, and they are no closer to their phonetic than ordinary pairs of that kind: initial and final both differ in 45 of them (56%), against 284 of 575 (49%; p = 0.34), and of those with OC forms at both ends, 6 of 29 are related by affix or regular alternation, against 48 of 179. Some are related in OC though not in MC, as 墨 \*C.mˤək 'ink' is to 黑 \*m̥ˤək 'black' (*mok*, *xok*). Many are not related in sound on any account, as 吏 *liH* 'official', glossed "one who governs men" (治人者也), is not to 史 *sriX* 'scribe'. Yet 10 of the 18 such labelled pairs in the blind sample are coded related in meaning, while 8 of the 33 labelled pairs there are coded unrelated.

### 4.5 Recensions, glosses and filing position (H3, H4)

The Xiǎo Xú separates the labels. Almost a third of the decidable labels (62 of 202) are the Dà Xú's alone, whether added there or lost from the Xiǎo Xú, and they are closer to their phonetic than the shared labels (Table 6): homophonous in 24 of 48 pairs against 35 of 122 (OR for shared relative to Dà Xú-only labels 0.39, 95% CI 0.19–0.80, p = 0.01, Holm about 0.03), remote in 16 against 63 (Fisher p = 0.04), with similar shares of departing-tone alternations. The difference in identity persists among labels not filed under their phonetic (24 of 46 against 26 of 90; p = 0.009). The Dà Xú-only labels also more often have paronomastic glosses (21 of 62 entries against 31 of 140; p = 0.08), while in meaning the two layers do not differ (8 of 10 against 16 of 22 related).

Paronomastic glosses mark a second gradient. Neither Duan's emendations nor filing position separates the labels. ## 5 Discussion

The hypotheses can be answered briefly. H1a: yes, also within series, although there the effect depends on the Dà Xú-only labels and the paronomastic glosses. H1b: no, and informatively for tone and voicing, since of those alternations only the departing tone sets labelled pairs apart; less informatively for other affixes. H1c: yes, but not as framed; the label goes with the \*-s relation, not with its direction. H2: yes, in every subset. Of the exploratory comparisons, only the Xiǎo Xú separates the labels. Of the accounts in Table 1, (B) fails on H1b and on direction. (D) cannot produce the contrast in meaning anywhere, or the contrast in sound across all labels; for the 92 core pairs it cannot be excluded on sound alone. (A) and (C) both fit, and the comparison that would separate them is too small to decide. What the label goes with can therefore be stated as a result. What its author had in mind cannot yet be.

### 5.1 Artefacts

Four artefacts could produce the homophony effect: Xu's habit of glossing a character by its own phonetic, additions by the Song editors of the Dà Xú, errors in matching fǎnqiè to *Guǎngyùn* readings, and a higher rate of homophony in labelled series. Each predicts that the effect vanishes under one check, and taken one at a time it survives each (Table 7). Taken together, the checks tell a less simple story. They overlap, since all use one text and one set of MC readings, and the OC \*-s trend largely re-counts the MC departing-tone pairs (Section 4.2). Either of the first two checks combined with the within-series comparison, or the two combined with each other, leaves the contrast in sound imprecise, while the contrast in meaning does not move.

### 5.2 Meaning first, or sound as well?

The two surviving accounts differ on one point: whether the label records a relation of meaning, from which its sound profile follows, or a relation between words, which fixes sound as well. The meaning-first account passes the tests available. Its precondition holds: relatedness predicts identity or a departing-tone alternation when the label is controlled (OR 10.1), and in both groups related pairs are more often identical or departing-tone alternants than unrelated ones, although the ordinary group has only six related pairs (Section 4.3). A label assigned for meaning alone would therefore produce much of the observed profile. The account also accommodates the remote labels, which (C) does not lead one to expect (Section 4.4). The gradient of Section 4.5 fits both: on (C), paronomastic glosses and Dà Xú-only labels mark the most obvious cases of one word written twice; on (A), they merely mark the most conspicuous of the meaningful phonetics.

What would separate the accounts is whether the label still goes with sound among pairs already related in meaning. On (C) it should, since among related pairs the label would single out the same word or a minimal derivative, which stands closer in sound to the phonetic than a remote cognate does; on (A) it need not. In this sample the label adds little once relatedness is known (OR 1.3), but with six related ordinary pairs that is absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

On balance, the label behaves as a record of relatedness of meaning, and its phonology is that of relatedness in these series: enriched in identity and the \*-s alternation, though nearly half of the related pairs are remote in sound. Whether the analyst also heard the two words as one, or as base and derivative, remains a hypothesis, worth stating because it generates tests.

> *The base-word hypothesis.* A label was written when the phonetic graph was recognised as writing the word on which the headword was built.

The hypothesis was formed after the results. It would be contradicted if the test above, run on all labelled pairs and a large sample of ordinary ones, found no difference. It also predicts that excavated manuscripts write labelled headwords with the bare phonetic graph more often than homophonous ordinary compounds (cf. Section 2.2), and that the *Shuōwén*'s "read like" notes (讀若) and sound glosses (聲訓) linking a compound to its own phonetic favour identity and the departing tone. A meaning-first label predicts none of this beyond what relatedness brings.

Why the departing tone, and not the other alternations? On the meaning-first reading the question moves from the label to the language: the \*-s derivatives in these series stayed related in meaning to their bases, as far as Xu's glosses show, while pairs linked by other alternations seldom did. That fits the standing of tone C as "the most common derivational device in OC" (Schuessler, 2007) and of the departing-tone alternation as the "least controversial" evidence of Old Chinese morphology (Zhang, 2022), and the finding that derivational processes differ in how predictable their semantics are, zero derivation not being by default the less predictable (Barbu Mititelu et al., 2026). But the numbers are small, and half the labelled \*-s members are glossed by their own phonetic, so the \*-s result is supported rather than secure.

### 5.3 Diagnosing derivation without overt morphology

To call two words derivationally related, one needs three things: their meanings must be related, their forms must stand in a relation that a derivational process could produce, and one must be derived from the other. Where affixes are overt, the form supplies the second and the third. Where they are not, as in conversion, all three must be argued, and relatedness is often established first (Ševčíková & Hledíková, 2026).

The results suggest a division of labour. The label supplies relatedness as a native analyst judged it, independently of our semantic coding, which never saw the label, though not of Xu's glosses (Section 6). The sound correspondence supplies the formal relation, independently of both. Direction comes from neither. The label is silent on it, and the graph's default, the compound as the derived member, is only partly specific to derivation (75% of pure departing-tone pairs against 61% where the syllables also differ otherwise; Section 4.2): the structure of the compound favours the derived reading without establishing it. Where relatedness and a regular formal relation coincide, as for 琀 and 珥, a derivational analysis is well supported. Where they part, as for 鬢 (a formal relation without relatedness) or 吏 (relatedness without a regular formal relation), it is not.

This is, on a larger scale, the lesson Rasin et al. (2024) drew from Arad's diagnostics (Section 2.3). It also fits paradigm-based definitions of relatedness, which require no designated base and no fixed direction (Bonami & Strnadová, 2019; Hathout & Namer, 2019). The label is a native judgement of the content side. Tested at the level of the population, it recovers the form relations that recur with that content, identity and the \*-s alternation, and nothing about direction, which is compatible with a paradigm-based view. Relatedness evidence, native or experimental, need not carry information about direction.

The homophonous labelled pairs show what the script adds. The phonology records nothing for them, yet the descriptive coding classes most as a sense given its own graph and five as conversion: the determinative marks the change of meaning without change of form that speech leaves invisible. A graphic device, not a morpheme, it shows only that the writers treated the word as distinct, but it is an overt record of a covert distinction, which diagnostics of conversion usually lack.

Whether the relations the label tracks reflect a word-based or a root-based grammar of Old Chinese, or only which relations had stayed transparent, the design cannot tell. It does bear on how derivational families are delimited. A phonetic series is a graphic set whose members may share no more than a sound; within it, the label favours a core of homophonous and \*-s-related pairs, set among loans and remote cognates. Families structured by recurrent form–meaning relations would correspond to this core, and a native label, once tested against a comparison group, offers a criterion for finding it.

### 5.4 Whose label?

The shared layer, the one most likely to go back to Xu Shen, is less often homophonous and more often remote than the Dà Xú-only layer (Table 6). If the Dà Xú-only labels are later additions, two readings fit. Whoever added them may have applied Xu's criterion more generously, so that the extra labels fell where related pairs are densest, on homophones. Or they may have followed a narrower, sound-oriented criterion, labelling where the phonetic sounded the same and the gloss used it (as 34% of the Dà Xú-only labels do, against 22% of the shared ones), while Xu's own labels rest on the broader judgement that the phonetic contributes meaning; much of the phonological core would then be a product of transmission. The *Shuōwén* alone cannot decide between them, but Tang witnesses, such as the manuscript fragment of the wood section, could date some of the differences. Either way the shared labels keep the contrast in meaning intact and a weaker contrast in sound, so the core is not solely an editor's creation.

Wang Yun thought that the Dà Xú had added labels, and 62 of its labels are indeed absent from the Xiǎo Xú. His rejections, however, do not pick out a different kind of relation. Seven of his nine fall on the same relations as the rest: 愾, 恇, 婚 and 姻 are homophonous with their phonetic, and 娶, 緉 and 坪 are its departing-tone members. Two of his differentiated graphs, 傾 'lean' and 娶 'take a wife', are both filed away from their base. He lets the label on 傾, homophonous with 頃, stand, and the Xiǎo Xú has it too; he rejects it on 娶, the departing-tone member beside 取 (*tshjuH*, *tshjuX*), where the Xiǎo Xú reads 取聲, calling the Dà Xú analysis "not the convention" (非例; Wang Yun, 1837, juan 8). In this pair, at least, what separates his decisions is the reading of the Xiǎo Xú, not the relation of sound. And one label that has served as evidence in OC reconstruction is among the unstable ones: 喪, a standard example of the prefix \*s- (Mei, 2012; Sagart & Baxter, 2012), is labelled only in the Dà Xú. The Xiǎo Xú still analyses it as a phonetic compound, so what is unstable is the label, not the phonetic relation.

## 6 Limitations

Three bridging assumptions carry the argument: that the MC readings reflect the sound relations of Xu's words, that his glosses reflect Han-period relations of meaning, and that the Dà Xú label is his judgement. The checks of Section 5.1 constrain each without proving it. The first is weakest where it matters most for H1c, since the reading tradition includes analogical creations and MC does not always show which words had \*-s (Baxter & Sagart, 1998, 2014).

The semantic coding cannot be wholly independent of Xu, because the glosses we coded were written in the same act as the label, and a gloss "could well have been suggested by the graphic element" (Schuessler, 2007). Removing paronomastic glosses reduces this dependence without eliminating it. Blindness, moreover, removed the label from the sheet but not from the coders' memory, and both authors, like the language model, may know the analysis of common characters. The two machine passes come from one model family, so their errors may be correlated. The sample was drawn from pairs with OC forms, which favours common characters, so its odds ratio is not a population-wide semantic rate, and it contains only six related ordinary pairs, too few for the comparison that would separate the accounts in Section 5.2.

The phonological coverage is uneven. OC forms cover a quarter of the pairs, which limits H1b; alternations between the departing and entering tones (\*-ks beside \*-k) and \*-s derivatives with different MC rhymes were not coded; and no comparison with an independent reconstruction such as Schuessler's (2007) has been made.

Finally, the text. The population was extracted from a digital edition, *xīnfù* were identified by a heuristic, and the check against the print covered 104 entries. Two discrepancies with the Chen scan, in 䢈 and 𨻺, remain unresolved.

## 7 Conclusion

Is the *Shuōwén*'s *yìshēng* label an implicit marker of derivation? Not as such. What it tracks most robustly is relatedness of meaning between the phonetic's word and the compound's, and in Old Chinese phonetic series relatedness is densest among homophones and pairs that differ by \*-s. Across all labels, the label therefore favours these two relations above baseline, passes over the other alternations found in the same series, and says nothing about direction. The sound profile is sharpest in the labels found only in the Dà Xú and in entries glossed by their own phonetic; in the plain labels most likely to be Xu Shen's own it is weak, while the relation of meaning is not. Wang Yun saw part of this in 1837. Tested on the whole population, his filing criterion does not separate the labels, and the labels he rejected fall on the same relations as the rest.

The study is also a proof of concept. A native metalinguistic label can be tested like any other classification, against a comparison group and against independent evidence of sound and meaning. When it passes, the test shows what it measures, here relatedness, and what it does not, here direction. The same test can be turned on the tradition's other devices, the 讀若 notes, the sound glosses and Duan's 之言 glosses, and on the writing of labelled words in excavated manuscripts.
