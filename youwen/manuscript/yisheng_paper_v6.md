**Abstract**

Native metalinguistic labels are an underused source of evidence for Old Chinese morphology. The *Shuōwén jiězì* 說文解字 (100 CE) marks some characters as *yìshēng* 亦聲, "[the meaningful component] is also the phonetic". Wang Yun 王筠 (1837) counted "differentiated graphs filed in their own section" (分別文之在本部者), new graphs for an existing word or one of its senses, as one kind of *yìshēng*, and rejected nine of the labels he discussed as additions to the Dà Xú recension. We compare all 212 *yìshēng* entries of the Dà Xú text, less Xu Xuan's additions, with the 953 ordinary phonetic compounds on the same 172 phonetics, using Middle Chinese readings, Baxter–Sagart reconstructions and a semantic coding of a random subsample made blind to the label. Labelled characters are homophonous with their phonetic more than twice as often (35.6% vs 15.5%; odds ratio [OR] 2.38, clustered by phonetic). Among pairs that differ only in tone or voicing, the labelled character more often carries the departing tone, the reflex of the suffix \*-s (48.5% vs 28.7%; OR 2.44). Labelled pairs are not more often related by other affixes or alternations. In the Old Chinese-covered blind subsample they are far more often related in meaning (76% vs 12%), even without glosses that define a character by its own phonetic. *Yìshēng* thus marks a new graph for the same word or for a minimally derived one, not affixal derivation in general. The pattern is not confined to Wang's third kind, and it holds on the 140 labels that the Xiǎo Xú recension shares. The 62 that only the Dà Xú carries are more often homophonous (50% vs 29%), which fits Wang's complaint that its editor labelled extended senses. The results also bear on the *yòuwén* 右文 tradition and on how word-derived formations are diagnosed in morphological theory.

**Keywords:** *Shuōwén jiězì*; *yìshēng*; Old Chinese morphology; derivation; phonetic compounds; Wang Yun; *yòuwén*

## 1. Introduction

Much of what is known about Old Chinese derivation comes, directly or indirectly, from native sources. The phonetic series on which the reconstruction of affixes such as \*s- partly rests (Sagart & Baxter 2012) are the work of the script's makers, and the tone-change readings that Jacques (2022) treats as evidence for morphological alternations were recorded by early commentators. Xu Shen 許慎 belongs in this company, yet he has usually been mined for data rather than questioned as a witness. His *Shuōwén jiězì* 說文解字 (100 CE; hereafter *Shuōwén*) analyses nearly ten thousand characters, and some of those analyses amount to judgements about how two words are related. Whether such judgements carry information about derivation is an empirical question, and this paper puts one of them to the test.

The judgement in question is the label *yìshēng* 亦聲, "also phonetic". An ordinary phonetic compound is analysed as 从A，B聲, "from A, with B as the phonetic". A *yìshēng* character is analysed instead as a union of two meaningful parts, one of which is then said to supply the sound as well: 从A从B，B亦聲. What the label covers is far from uniform:

| (1) a. 禮 *lejX* "rite", glossed 所以事神致福也 "that by which the spirits are served and blessings obtained"; phonetic 豊 *lejX* "ritual vessel"
|       b. 殯 *pjinH* "to lay out the encoffined dead"; phonetic 賓 *pjin* "guest"
|       c. 貧 *bin* "poor", glossed 財分少也 "wealth divided and therefore little"; phonetic 分 *pjun* "to divide"

In (1a) the two words are homophonous in Middle Chinese (MC); in (1b) they differ in tone alone; in (1c) initial and rhyme both differ, though Xu's gloss ties the meanings tightly, and the label itself is found only in the Dà Xú recension, the Xiǎo Xú having plain 分聲. Wang Yun, as we shall see, accepted the label on 禮 and rejected the one on 貧. So what, if anything, does the label pick out, and whose judgement is it?

Traditional scholarship has mostly asked something else, namely where such characters belong among the "six scripts" (六書). The one scholar who came close to a claim about words was Wang Yun 王筠 (1837). Among his three kinds of *yìshēng* are "differentiated graphs filed in their own section" (分別文之在本部者; Wang 1837, juan 3, 10a/7–8), a differentiated graph being a new graph for a word or for one of its senses, and of the thirteen labels he discusses he rejects nine as additions to the Dà Xú text. He gave no counts, however, and did not compare the sounds of the two words.

We test Wang's idea on the whole population. Every *yìshēng* entry of the Dà Xú text, less the characters Xu Xuan added, is compared with the ordinary phonetic compounds built on the same phonetics, and the comparison asks whether the label covaries with evidence of a derivational relation gathered independently of it: Middle Chinese readings, Old Chinese reconstructions, and a semantic coding made blind to the label. The label, it turns out, tracks identity of sound and the departing-tone relation that reflects the Old Chinese suffix \*-s, but not the many other affixes and alternations found in the same series. Labelled pairs are also far more often related in meaning, and not merely because Xu so often defines a character by its own phonetic. *Yìshēng* marks a new graph for the same word, or for a minimally derived one. That is close to what Wang called differentiation, but it is not concentrated where his rule puts the label, in graphs filed under their base, and it holds both for the labels he rejected as later additions and for those the two recensions share.

Besides giving Wang's account its first population-level, phonologically controlled test, the results separate graphic differentiation of one word and derivation by \*-s from affixal derivation in general. The label supplies a native hypothesis about the relation between two words; independently coded meanings and sound correspondences test that hypothesis, a point that bears on a live debate in morphological theory (Arad 2003; Rasin, Preminger & Pesetsky 2024).

## 2. Background

### 2.1 From Duan Yucai to Wang Yun

In the Dà Xú 大徐 recension, as represented in the open digital edition used here, 227 head entries carry the formula; Section 3.1 explains why 212 of them are analysed. The canonical reading is Duan Yucai's 段玉裁. "Whenever [Xu] says 'also phonetic'," he writes in the first chapter of his commentary (卷一上), "the character is a meaning-compound that is at the same time a phonetic compound" (凡言亦聲者，會意兼形聲也). Duan did not think Xu had applied the label consistently. Of 論 "to discuss" and 議 "to deliberate", both given by Xu as ordinary phonetic compounds, he says the analysis "should read" 从言侖、侖亦聲 and 从言義、義亦聲, and his commentary goes further still, calling characters "phonetic compounds that contain a meaning-compound" (形聲包會意) 140 times in the digital edition. For Duan the label was the visible tip of a much larger pattern in which the phonetic also carries meaning. The sources and limits of this theory of a common origin for sound and meaning (聲義同源) are analysed by Xu Shushi (2024).

Modern studies have largely kept to Duan's question of membership. Hsu Yu-lung's (2005) thesis, the only monograph-length treatment we know of, collects 29 earlier interpretations and, according to its abstract, analyses 267 *yìshēng* characters across three versions of the text; Li Ning and Guo Shuyuan (2019) list 79 characters that in their view should have carried the label.

Wang Yun's contribution is of a different kind, and it is the one this paper builds on. A whole section of juan 3 of his *Shuōwén shìlì* 說文釋例 (1837) is devoted to the label, and it opens with a classification:

> 言亦聲者凡三種：會意字而兼聲者，一也；形聲字而兼意者，二也；分別文之在本部者，三也。
>
> "*Yìshēng* is said of three kinds: meaning-compounds that also indicate the sound; phonetic compounds that also indicate the meaning; and differentiated graphs filed in their own section." (Wang 1837, juan 3, 10a/7–8)

The differentiated graph (*fēnbiéwén* 分別文) is defined in juan 8. When a component added to an existing graph changes its meaning (其加偏旁而義遂異者), the result is a differentiated graph, of which there are two sorts: either the original sense of the base had been usurped by a borrowed one and the new component keeps them apart, or the base had many senses and the new graph takes only one. When the meaning stays the same, the result is an "accumulated graph" (*lěizēngzì* 累增字) (juan 8, 1a–1b). The definition never mentions the label. What joins the two is a rule of filing, stated right after the three kinds: "differentiated graphs are not uniform: those filed in another section never state [the base's] meaning; those in their own section always make the principal component the sound as well" (若分別文則不概：在異部者，概不言義；在本部者，概以主義兼聲也; juan 3, 10a/9–10b/2). The principal component must be the one that heads the section, to judge from Wang's one worked example of the third kind. Xu files 胖 "half a carcass" under 半 "half" rather than under 肉 "meat", and Wang reads the choice as "Xu's intention to differentiate" (許君分別之意; 11a/7–8); the second kind he never illustrates. The other half of the rule appears in juan 8, where 援 is analysed as plain 爰聲, without "from 爰", "to show that they are one word" (以著其爲一字也; 1b).

Nor did he take the transmitted labels as he found them. Of the thirteen labelled characters his section discusses, he accepts four (禮, 祏, 胖, 柵) and rejects nine (貧, 愾, 恇, 娶, 婚, 姻, 婢, 緉, 坪). His reason, given under 貧, is semantic: "extended and borrowed meanings are all folded into the phonetic, and often the Xiǎo Xú text does not spell them out where the Dà Xú text does" (凡引申假借之義，皆併入聲中，往往小徐不出而大徐出之; 13a/5–6). For five of the nine he names Xiǎo Xú's plain phonetic as correct, and the section closes on a verdict: "all cases of this kind are mistaken additions in the Dà Xú" (凡此類皆大徐誤增; 15a/9). He was not simply deferring to Xiǎo Xú, since he defends 柵 against its 冊聲 and rejects the label on 婢, which both recensions share. Juan 8 applies the same test. 娶 "to take a wife" is "the differentiated graph of 取", but Dà Xú's 从取，取亦聲 is "not the convention" (非例), and 婚 and 媄 fare the same (7b). Yet 傾 "to lean" and 𨻺 (U+28EFA), both 从頃，頃亦聲, and 䫇 (冄亦聲) he quotes without objection (6a–6b), although none is filed under its base and his own rule would expect a plain phonetic.

Two further remarks bear on what follows. Wang did not confine meaning to the labels: "a component that is also the phonetic always carries meaning; abbreviated and plain phonetics often do too" (亦聲必兼意；省聲及但言聲者，亦多兼意; juan 3, 1a). And his categories should not be conflated with the exegetical notion of "ancient and modern graphs" (古今字), a conflation that Jiang Zhiyuan (2021) traces to Xu Hao 徐灝 rather than to Wang himself (see also O 2015). What Wang offered was a normative account of when the label belongs, not a count of how often it coincides with differentiation, or with what relation of sound.

Behind all of this lies the older and wider debate over *yòuwén* 右文, the claim, first recorded in Shen Kuo's 沈括 *Mèngxī bǐtán* (c. 1088), that the right-hand component of a character carries meaning. Qing scholarship split it into two programmes. Duan's formula 凡从某聲皆有某義 attached meaning to the phonetic graph; Wang Niansun 王念孫 (1796), seeking ancient meanings through ancient sounds "unconstrained by graphic form" (不限形體), attached it to the spoken word. Shen Jianshi (1933) separated phonetics that signal meaning from phonetics borrowed for their sound, and Wang Li (1982) objected that *yòuwén* organises etymology by graph instead of by word. Current work speaks of the "source-indicating function" (示源功能) of the phonetic (Zeng 2002; Li 2020; Chen 2021), and a review of Chen (2021) names "substituting the character for the word" (以字代詞) as the central weakness of the whole approach (Chen Shuo 2022).

What this tradition lacks is not insight but measurement. It classifies characters instead of testing relations between words, it reports no baselines, and it leaves the phonology at "the same or close in sound" (音同音近), never asking whether a given sound difference is of the kind a morphological process would produce.

### 2.2 Derivation in Old Chinese

That last question belongs to historical phonology. Since Karlgren (1934) described Chinese vocabulary as organised into word families and Pulleyblank (1973, 2000) reinterpreted the sound differences within them as traces of affixes, reconstructions of Old Chinese have come to include explicit derivational morphology (Baxter & Sagart 1998, 2014; Sagart 1999; Schuessler 2007). Two affixes matter here. The suffix \*-s, reflected as the Middle Chinese departing tone (去聲), formed nouns from verbs (Mei 2012), and it may in fact be several suffixes, with nominalising, argument-demoting and adverbialising functions (Jacques 2016; Zhang 2022). The prefix \*s- had causative functions; Sagart and Baxter (2012) reconstruct it from phonetic-series evidence combined with word-family semantics.

Native material is thus already part of the evidence: Jacques (2022) defends the commentators' tone-change readings (破讀), and Hill and List (2019) turn the *Shuōwén*'s graphic analyses into a directed graph with which to test hypotheses about Old Chinese phonology. The *Shuōwén*'s own labels, by contrast, have not been examined as evidence of derivation.

### 2.3 Graph or word?

On its face, a *yìshēng* label is a statement about a graph, and for Qiu (2000) "meaning-compound-cum-phonetic" characters are simply a subtype of phonetic compounds. Boltz (1994), however, suggests why such a label might track something about words. On his account phonetic compounds arose when a graph extended to write a related word was disambiguated by an added signific, so that the phonetic of a derived word is often the graph of its base: precisely the configuration *yìshēng* describes, and in effect a history of Wang Yun's differentiated graphs. Because early scribes varied their choice of components considerably (Galambos 2006), the configurations preserved in the *Shuōwén* are partly a Han standardisation. A graphic label can count as evidence about morphology, then, only if it covaries with independent linguistic evidence.

Morphological theory tells us what contrast to look for. Arad (2003) distinguishes root-derived formations, which take unpredictable meanings from a category-neutral root, from word-derived ones, which inherit the meaning of an existing word; Rasin, Preminger and Pesetsky (2024) find that in Arad's own Hebrew data the semantic criterion fails to separate the two. Lexeme-based approaches take words as the units of derivation (Aronoff 2007) and treat derivational families as structured sets (Hathout & Namer 2019; Bonami & Strnadová 2019).

One more constraint comes from work on lexical systematicity. Since weak form–meaning systematicity exists across lexicons even without derivation (Monaghan et al. 2014; Dingemanse et al. 2015), the Chinese rhyme-book tradition included (Meng, Wan & Kit 2025), any effect of the label must be measured against the other members of the same phonetic series.

### 2.4 Hypotheses

Four directional hypotheses and two exploratory comparisons organise the analysis. The hypotheses were refined after an initial look at this population and were not preregistered.

- **H1a (homophony).** Labelled characters are homophonous with their phonetic more often than unlabelled members of the same series.
- **H1b (affixal regularity).** Among pairs that are not homophonous, labelled characters differ from their phonetic by a regular affix or alternation more often than unlabelled members.
- **H1c (the departing tone).** Among pairs that differ only in tone or in the voicing of the initial, the labelled character is the departing-tone (\*-s) member more often than in unlabelled pairs.
- **H2 (semantic relatedness).** The meaning of a labelled character is related to that of its phonetic more often than for unlabelled members, even without paronomastic glosses.
- **H3 (label stability; exploratory).** Items on which the Dà Xú text, the Xiǎo Xú 小徐 text and Duan's emended text disagree differ from stable items on H1a.
- **H4 (the filing criterion of Wang Yun's third kind; exploratory).** Labelled characters filed in the section headed by their own phonetic differ on H1a from the other labelled characters.

The split within H1 is the heart of the design. Identity is what accumulated graphs, differentiated graphs and conversion would produce; an affixal difference is what derivation proper would produce. H3, though exploratory, carries more weight than its label suggests, since Wang had already judged some labels to be additions to the Dà Xú text.

## 3. Data and method

### 3.1 Population and comparison group

The working text is the open digital *Shuōwén* of the shuowenjiezi project (GitHub, Apache-2.0): 9,833 head entries with the Dà Xú fǎnqiè and Duan's commentary. We checked the Dà Xú glosses and structural formulas quoted in the key examples character by character against a scan of Chen Changzhi's 1873 edition in the Waseda University Library; the checked passages and scan locations are recorded in the source-check ledger. The 1963 Zhonghua edition reproduces this edition at reduced size; our direct check was of the Waseda scan, not a physical copy of the Zhonghua printing. A pattern search finds the *yìshēng* formula in 224 head entries, and three more (從, 𠔁 [U+20501], 兩) were resolved by hand. Of these 227, four are false hits in which 亦 is itself the phonetic ("from X, 亦 phonetic"); they were moved to the ordinary compounds of 亦. Eleven more are *xīnfù* 新附 characters added by Xu Xuan 徐鉉 in 986. These were identified as the entries at the end of a section that lack Duan's commentary, since Duan omits the *xīnfù*; the rule finds 398 such entries in the whole book, close to the roughly 400 usually counted, and its one false positive, 否, which Duan keeps under 不, was restored. Formulas inside the glosses of variant graphs, and all abbreviated phonetics (省聲), were set aside. What remains is 212 labelled entries on 172 phonetics (Table 1).

The comparison group consists of every ordinary compound (X聲) on the same 172 phonetics after the same exclusions, 953 entries in all. Keeping only phonetics with at least one labelled member guarantees that both groups come from the same series, which matters because homophony with the phonetic is far more likely inside a series than between random pairs.

One subset needs separate treatment. Forty labelled entries are filed in the section headed by their own phonetic, as 從 is filed under 从 and 兩 under 㒳. By Wang Yun's own rule a differentiated graph filed in its base's section always carries the label, and his one example, 胖 under 半, is among these 40; they are therefore the candidates for his third kind. H4 tests this subset only, not differentiation as a whole. Their label also follows from Xu's filing system, a section head being by definition the meaningful component, and no ordinary compound is ever filed this way. Every main model is therefore also run without them (H4).

For H3, each labelled entry was collated with the Xiǎo Xú recension (*Shuōwén jiězì xìzhuàn* 說文解字繫傳) in the Sibu congkan edition, read in its digital text (Kanseki Repository KR1j0019) and, for the pages that the digital text omits, in the National Library of China scan of the same edition. Entries were located by unique text anchors, and Xu Shen's analysis, before Xu Kai's comment (臣鍇曰), was read for the label. Recorded readings from the digital text were checked against that text; readings transcribed from the scan were checked by sampling. Of the 212 labels, 140 appear in both recensions and 62 in the Dà Xú alone. Ten cannot be decided. Seven lie in juan 25, which the Xiǎo Xú lost and later supplied from the Dà Xú; two, 詔 and 糶, have fǎnqiè ending in 切 rather than the Xiǎo Xú's usual 反 and look supplied the same way; and in one, 瑁, the line is blank in the print.

### 3.2 Sound

The primary tests use Middle Chinese. Each character's Dà Xú fǎnqiè was matched against the *Guǎngyùn* 廣韻 readings in the open tshet-uinh database (GitHub, nk2028) and written in Baxter–Sagart (2014) MC notation. For each pair of member and phonetic, three variables were derived: whether the syllables are identical; whether they differ in tone and/or voicing of the initial and in nothing else; and, if so, which member carries the departing tone. Because the *Guǎngyùn* matching is itself an inference, identity was also coded directly as identity of the two fǎnqiè strings.

Why MC first? Partly for coverage: MC readings exist at both ends for 89% of pairs, Old Chinese reconstructions for only 27%. Partly to avoid circularity, since Baxter–Sagart reconstructions draw on phonetic-series evidence and MC readings do not. And the two relations of main interest, identity and the departing tone, are visible in MC anyway.

Old Chinese forms (Baxter & Sagart 2014, from the open cddb database, GitHub digling/cddb) serve a narrower purpose, typing the affixes that MC does not show. Each pair was classed as identical (I); different only in prefixes, suffixes (including \*-ʔ and \*-s), pharyngealisation or medial \*r (R, a regular affix or alternation); or different in the initial at the same place (O), at a different place (O2), in the vowel (V) or in the coda (C).

### 3.3 Meaning

Xu wrote the gloss and assigned the label in a single act, and this is the central difficulty for any semantic test. In 58 of the 212 labelled entries (27.4%) the definition contains the phonetic character itself, as in 政 正也 or 枰 平也; among ordinary compounds the figure is 11 of 953 (1.2%). A test based on Xu's glosses alone would partly measure his glossing habits. We therefore flag these paronomastic glosses, rerun every test without them, and code meaning on a sheet from which Xu's structural analysis has been removed.

The sheet holds a random sample of 100 pairs (40 labelled, 60 ordinary) drawn from pairs with Old Chinese forms at both ends. For each pair it shows only the phonetic character and the member character with their *Shuōwén* glosses, stripped of 从某, 某聲 and 某亦聲. The two authors independently coded the relation between the two basic meanings (本義) as Y (same or near-synonymous), E (one step of extension), N (no relation) or X (proper name), without seeing whether a pair was labelled. They then compared their assignments and jointly checked the final codes against the source glosses. Their assignments agreed on the 100 sampled pairs before reconciliation. Five sampled items were subsequently excluded as *xīnfù* or false hits and one as a mispaired entry (弈/廾), leaving 94 valid pairs. H2 uses the authors' agreed blind codes; removing 11 proper-name cases leaves 83 for its primary test.

A further coding, used only descriptively, assigned each of the 196 MC-homophonous pairs a relation type: the same word with an added determinative (累增字, L), one sense given its own graph (分別文, F), a change of word class (conversion, C), an unrelated homophone or phonetic loan (U), or a proper name (X). Both authors reviewed these assignments pair by pair against the glosses and agreed on the final types. The label was visible in this descriptive coding. These categories borrow Wang's terms but not his criteria. Ours classify the relation between the two words, his what the added component does to the base graph, and the two can disagree: 傾, which Wang counts as a differentiated graph of 頃, was coded L. L and F together are therefore the closer counterpart of Wang's two categories.

### 3.4 Models

The main analysis uses one model family: logistic generalised estimating equations (GEE) for each binary outcome, with the label as predictor and observations clustered by phonetic under an exchangeable working correlation. We report odds ratios and 95% confidence intervals. For the five directional comparisons (H1a; H1b in MC and in OC; H1c; H2), one-sided p-values are adjusted by Holm's method. Fisher's exact tests describe small subsets, including phonetics with both labelled and ordinary members. The models were fitted in Python (statsmodels). GEE adjusts uncertainty for dependence within phonetic series; it does not eliminate differences between series in their baseline propensity for homophony. The matched-series and recension-restricted comparisons therefore assess whether that difference drives the main result.

Because the hypotheses were refined after exploratory inspection of the same finite population, these tests describe the observed pattern rather than an independent confirmation. We report the null results and the unplanned decomposition of H1c, and use the blind author coding and the Duan and Xiǎo Xú layers as separate checks.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Homophony (H1a)

The clearest result is also the simplest. More than a third of the labelled characters with MC readings (63 of 177, 35.6%) are homophonous with their phonetic, against 15.5% of ordinary compounds on the same phonetics (133 of 858). Clustering by phonetic barely dents the effect (OR 2.38, 95% CI 1.69–3.34; Holm p = 1.2 × 10⁻⁶), and none of the checks in Table 2 does either. At the OC level the contrast is sharper still, 25.0% against 5.2%, though on far smaller numbers.

### 4.2 Other affixes and alternations (H1b)

H1b fails. Among non-identical pairs, 40.0% of labelled and 35.6% of ordinary compounds differ from their phonetic by an OC affix or regular alternation alone (OR 1.17, Holm p = 0.31); within the matched phonetics the order is even reversed (39.4% against 46.5%), and no affix subtype contains more than two labelled pairs. The MC figures look more favourable at first, with tone or voicing alternations in 28.9% of labelled against 20.7% of ordinary non-identical pairs, but the difference does not survive clustering (OR 1.29, one-sided p = 0.10, Holm p = 0.20). Nor does it reflect a general excess. Once the departing-tone members discussed in Section 4.3 are set aside, the remaining alternations sit exactly at baseline, 14.9% against 14.8% (Table 3).

### 4.3 The departing tone (H1c)

Where member and phonetic differ only in tone or voicing, the labelled character is the departing-tone member in 16 of 33 cases (48.5%) and the ordinary compound in 43 of 150 (28.7%). The difference holds under clustering (OR 2.44, 95% CI 1.34–4.48; Holm p = 0.0056) and without the 40 labels filed under their own phonetic (OR 2.58, Holm p = 0.004). Among the labelled cases are 殯 *pjinH* beside 賓 *pjin*, 琀 *homH* beside 含 *hom*, 憙 "to delight in" *xiH* beside 喜 "joy" *xiX*, and 珥 "ear-jade" *nyiH* beside 耳 "ear" *nyiX*; all four keep the label in the Xiǎo Xú and in Duan as well.

H1c, as stated, is supported. But what exactly is the label selecting?

We framed the hypothesis on the assumption that the label picks out the derived member of a pair, and a decomposition that we did not plan in advance shows that assumption to be wrong (Table 4). Labelled alternations do involve the departing tone more often than ordinary ones, 21 of 33 against 58 of 150 (OR 2.42). Once a pair involves the departing tone at all, however, the direction is the same in both groups: the compound, not the phonetic, is the departing-tone member in 16 of 21 labelled pairs (76%) and in 43 of 58 ordinary pairs (74%; Fisher p = 1.0, two-sided). Across the script, then, when a compound and its phonetic differ by \*-s, the compound is usually the \*-s form, whether or not Xu labelled it. What sets labelled pairs apart is the frequency of the \*-s relation, not its direction.

### 4.4 Meaning (H2)

Meaning shows the largest contrast of all. In the authors' blind coding, with proper names set aside, 25 of 33 labelled pairs (75.8%) are related in meaning (Y or E), against 6 of 50 ordinary pairs (12.0%): an odds ratio of 22.0 (95% CI 7.1–68.0; Holm p = 2.0 × 10⁻⁷). Removing the paronomastic glosses lowers it only to 16.4 (18 of 26 against 6 of 50). Near-synonymy (Y) occurs among labelled pairs alone, 8 of 33 against none of 50 (Table 5).

How do sound and meaning combine? In the subsample, all nine homophonous labelled pairs are related in meaning, and none of the eight unrelated labelled pairs is homophonous. When both the label and relatedness enter a descriptive logistic model of MC identity (n = 83), relatedness predicts identity (OR 6.3, p = 0.04) while the label adds little once relatedness is known (OR 1.4, p = 0.69). This small, selected subsample supports the joint sound-and-meaning interpretation, but the regression does not establish that meaning causes homophony.

The descriptive relation types agree. Of the 63 homophonous labelled pairs, the authors classed 42 as a sense given its own graph (分別文), 8 as the same word with an added determinative (累增字), 5 as conversion and only 8 (13%) as unrelated homophones; among the 133 homophonous ordinary pairs, unrelated homophones (73, 55%) and proper names (42, 32%) predominate.

### 4.5 Recensions and filing position (H3, H4)

Of the three exploratory comparisons, only the one with the Xiǎo Xú finds a difference. Duan keeps the label on 170 of the 212 entries and drops it from 39 (three have no Duan text in the digital edition), yet the entries he drops are, if anything, more often homophonous than those he keeps: 13 of 32 against 49 of 143 (41% against 34%; Fisher p = 0.54). Two thirds of the labels he drops (26 of 39) are missing from the Xiǎo Xú as well, among them 旄 "banner of yak tails" and 禬 "a sacrifice to gather blessings". Duan's text gives them as 毛聲 and 會聲, as the Xiǎo Xú does, but his notes on both call them 形聲包會意; what he changed was the wording of the analysis, not his view of the words.

The Xiǎo Xú gives a larger and sharper test. Almost a third of the decidable labels (62 of 202) are the Dà Xú's alone, which bears out in numbers Wang's remark that the Xiǎo Xú "often does not spell out" what the Dà Xú does. The added labels are not weaker than the shared ones; they are closer to their phonetic. Half of those with MC readings are homophonous with it (24 of 48), against 29% of the shared labels (35 of 122; GEE OR 0.39, 95% CI 0.19–0.80, two-sided p = 0.01; Holm about 0.03). Tone and voicing alternations, by contrast, are equally common in both (p = 0.63), and in the blind subsample the added labels are as often related in meaning (8 of 10 against 16 of 22). On the 140 shared labels alone, all three main associations survive Holm correction (Tables 2, 4 and 5). H1c is again the weakest, but on 24 labelled pairs its interval lies clear of 1 (OR 2.44, 95% CI 1.14–5.24).

The collation also checks Wang. Each Xiǎo Xú reading he cites agrees with the Sibu congkan text in lacking the label, though not always in wording: for 婚 (juan 3, 14a/3) and 像 (juan 8, 6a/4) he reports 昏聲 and 象聲, where the print has no phonetic at all (从女昏; 從人象). 愾, 恇 and 婢, which he also rejects, carry the label in both recensions; there he was applying his semantic criterion, not recension evidence. Removing all nine of his rejected labels leaves H1a and H2 intact and H1c weaker but standing (OR 2.11, one-sided p = 0.014).

Filing position shows nothing of the kind. The 40 labels filed under their own phonetic, the candidates for Wang's third kind, are less, not more, often homophonous than the others (9 of 34, 26.5%, against 54 of 143, 37.8%; Fisher p = 0.24, two-sided).

### 4.6 The series of 賓

A single series shows these patterns at close range. Xu labels 殯 (*pjinH*), whose gloss explains that the coffined dead is "treated as a guest" (賓遇之). Two ordinary compounds on 賓 *pjin* are also read *pjinH*. One is 儐 "to usher [guests]" (導也), which Duan connects with treating someone by the rites due to a guest (取賓禮相待之義); the other is 鬢 "hair on the cheeks", which has nothing to do with guests and which Duan relates instead to 濱 "edge" (鬢者、髮之濱也). All three are departing-tone forms beside a level-tone phonetic, as Section 4.3 leads one to expect of compounds in general. Only 殯 carries the label, though 儐 is also a candidate for \*-s derivation from 賓 on the available sound and meaning evidence. The label is thus an informative but non-exhaustive indicator, just as Duan's "should read … also phonetic" notes and the 79 candidates of Li and Guo (2019) imply.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 What *yìshēng* marks

The label preferentially marks characters whose phonetic writes the same word, or a word from which the labelled word is minimally derived. In the latter cases, converging sound and meaning evidence permits a derivational interpretation of *yìshēng*, rather than treating it only as a graphic classification. That is the central claim of this paper.

Three kinds of evidence converge on it: identity of sound, the departing tone and relatedness of meaning. The wider class of affixal relations (\*s-, \*N-, \*m-, \*-ʔ, the type A/B contrast) that links so many members of a phonetic series to one another does not set labelled pairs apart.

Within this class, three relations can be told apart. By far the most frequent is identity of sound together with relatedness of meaning, which is what Wang Yun's accumulated and differentiated graphs describe: 返 "to return" is read exactly like 反 "to turn over" (*pjonX*), and 鉤 "hook" not only sounds like 句 (*kuw*) but carries its very gloss, 曲也 "bent". Conversion, a change of word class without change of form, is rarer, with five homophonous labelled pairs in the author-verified coding and none among ordinary ones. Derivation by \*-s, as in 殯 beside 賓 or 憙 beside 喜, makes up the rest. Only this last relation is derivation in the phonological sense. The other two are, in the terms of Section 2.3, graphic events: the word stays what it was, and the script gives it, or one of its senses, a new shape.

Wang described much of this in 1837, and two of his differentiated graphs show both relations at once. 傾 is read exactly like 頃 (*khjwieng*). 娶 "to take a wife" is the departing-tone member beside 取 "to take" (*tshjuH* beside *tshjuX*), the relation tested in H1c, and Wang still calls it a differentiated graph. Where his graphic category treats the two alike, the sound relations tell them apart, and the label is sensitive to both. What the population adds is scale. On the author-verified coding, a new graph for the same word or for one of its senses (L or F) accounts for 50 of the 63 homophonous labelled pairs, against 18 of the 133 homophonous ordinary ones, while the filing criterion of his third kind does not pick out homophony at all (Section 4.5). Differentiation runs through the label, not through one kind of it.

The departing-tone result also corrects our starting assumption, that the label picks out the derived member of a pair. It has no need to, because the script already does: in both groups, when a compound and its phonetic differ by \*-s, the compound is the \*-s form about three times in four, and Boltz's (1994) account explains why, since the added signific is what creates the graph of the new word. The label's contribution lies elsewhere. It singles out pairs whose relation is minimal, identity or \*-s, and in doing so it records a recognition, whether Xu Shen's or an editor's (Section 5.2), that in these characters the phonetic is no mere sound sign but the graph of the base word.

### 5.2 Whose label?

Wang Yun would have read these results differently. On his rule a differentiated graph filed outside its base's section takes a plain phonetic, yet 42 of the 50 homophonous labelled pairs that the authors classified as L or F are filed outside it. Either the Dà Xú departs from Xu Shen's convention on a large scale, as Wang believed, or the convention is partly Wang's own. The collation gives each answer a share. The added labels are indeed where his rule says no label belongs: only 3 of the 62 are filed under their base, against 37 of the 140 shared ones (partly because filing under the phonetic leaves no other way to write the analysis), and all 22 of their homophonous L or F pairs are filed elsewhere. But the shared labels break the rule too, in 18 of their 26 such pairs, and Wang himself lets 傾, 𨻺 (U+28EFA) and 䫇 keep labels that his rule would withhold.

He was right, too, that the Dà Xú labels more freely, and right about where. Of the 24 homophonous pairs whose label is the Dà Xú's alone, 19 are, in the author-verified coding, graphs that give one sense of a word its own shape (F), against 21 of the 35 shared ones. These are the "extended meanings" that, on his account, Xu Shen folded into the phonetic, and a graph made for one sense of a word should share its sound. Whoever added these labels, Xu Xuan or a copyist before him, marked the relation that the shared labels mark, only more generously. The additions inflate the homophony effect but do not create the pattern of Section 5.1: the shared labels show it too. So do the labels Wang rejected, which fall mostly on identity and \*-s, because what decides a case for him is meaning, an original sense against an extended or borrowed one, and that criterion cuts across the sound relations.

What no recension can exclude is that some labels entered the text before either Xu edited it.

### 5.3 Roots, words and the diagnosis of derivation

For morphological theory the Chinese case is useful because it pulls relatedness apart from affixation. Old Chinese had no inflection that could expose zero derivation, and several of its derivational affixes left no direct trace in the Middle Chinese syllable. The script, however, marks sense specialisation and conversion by adding a determinative, and the label records a recognition of that configuration. Graphic evidence can thus reveal a kind of word-based derivation that phonology alone would hide.

The design also sidesteps the difficulty that Rasin, Preminger and Pesetsky (2024) raise for Arad (2003): if unpredictable meaning cannot separate root-derived from word-derived formations, some other criterion is needed. Here the transmitted label supplies a classification external to the phonological tests. The authors' separate blind semantic coding then establishes which sampled pairs have related meanings. In these data, the label is associated with both semantic relatedness and the two narrow sound relations, identity and \*-s. A formal diagnostic of direction is the one thing the design cannot supply, since direction, as Section 4.3 showed, is shared by labelled and unlabelled compounds alike.

The units that pattern, finally, are words and their graphic or derivational families, which is naturally stated in lexeme-based terms (Aronoff 2007; Hathout & Namer 2019; Bonami & Strnadová 2019). A root-based account would have to explain why the label ignores the affixal relations that connect other members of the same series to the same root.

### 5.4 *Yòuwén* and the source-indicating phonetic

The phonetic "carries meaning" in two quite different ways, and the *yòuwén* tradition runs them together. Where it writes the same word or its \*-s derivative, meaning is shared strongly, and this is where the labels concentrate. In the selected ordinary pairs, six of fifty non-name cases show a Y or E relation. That estimate cannot be projected to every ordinary compound; it is compatible with weak systematicity found across lexicons (Monaghan et al. 2014; Meng, Wan & Kit 2025); Wang Yun's remark that plain phonetics "often carry meaning too" anticipates it. The formula 凡从某聲皆有某義 treats the two situations as one. Separating them gives a quantitative footing to the charge that *yòuwén* substitutes the character for the word (Wang 1982; Chen Shuo 2022), and it locates the "source-indicating function" of the phonetic (Chen 2021) in a subset of each series that the label marks.

## 6. Limitations

The main semantic limitation is the sampling frame. The blind subsample was drawn from pairs with Old Chinese forms at both ends, favouring common characters; its large estimated odds ratio does not establish a population-wide semantic rate. The authors independently coded the 100 blind pairs and jointly reviewed the homophonous relation types. Their separate coding records were not retained, so the sequence of individual decisions cannot be reconstructed from the released final codes. The 196 relation types were assigned with the labels visible and remain descriptive rather than an independent test.

The phonological evidence has gaps of its own. MC readings are *Guǎngyùn* positions matched to Dà Xú fǎnqiè and therefore postdate Xu Shen, and some tone-change readings may be later innovations; the fǎnqiè-string check rules out the matching procedure as the source of H1a, but not the chronological gap. Old Chinese forms cover only a quarter of the pairs and depend partly on phonetic-series evidence, which limits H1b. Alternations between the departing and entering tones (\*-ks beside \*-k) and possible \*-s derivatives with different MC rhymes were not coded, so H1c applies only to the specified narrow contrast, and no comparison with an independent reconstruction such as Schuessler (2007) has yet been made.

Three smaller caveats remain. The Dà Xú population was extracted from a digital text and *xīnfù* were identified by a heuristic. The selected Dà Xú passages listed in the source-check ledger were checked against the scanned Chen Changzhi edition, but a character-by-character collation of all 212 population entries against the print remains to be done. Ten labels stay outside the Xiǎo Xú comparison: nine stand in text that the Xiǎo Xú took from the Dà Xú (the seven of juan 25 and, probably, 詔 and 糶), so no copy of it could settle them, and the tenth, 瑁, would need an edition other than the Sibu congkan, in which its line is blank; the scan readings, moreover, were checked only by sampling. And because the hypotheses were refined after an exploratory look, every test is reported, the null for H1b and the unplanned decomposition of H1c included.

## 7. Conclusion

Is the *Shuōwén*'s *yìshēng* label an implicit marker of derivation? Only in part. It preferentially marks the same word under a new graph and can mark derivation by \*-s; other affixal relations do not show the same association. Wang Yun recognised much of this when he made differentiated graphs filed in their own section a kind of *yìshēng*. Tested on the whole population, differentiation turns out not to depend on filing: it runs through the label as a whole, and part of it proves to be derivational. Almost a third of the decidable labels are the Dà Xú's alone, as Wang suspected, and they lean further towards homophony; but the pattern holds on the labels that both recensions share, so it is not an artefact of the Song edition.

The labels can therefore join commentarial readings and phonetic series as native evidence on word-based derivation, and they mark the subset of each phonetic series in which, for the *yòuwén* tradition, the phonetic really does carry meaning. The same design could be turned on the tradition's other metalinguistic devices: the 讀若 "read like" notes, the sound glosses (聲訓) and Duan's 之言 glosses.
